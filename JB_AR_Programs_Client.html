Leg press – high foot placement
3 x 8Leg press machineModerate
Logged weight (if necessary)✓

Start: Sit in the leg press with the feet placed high on the platform, hip to shoulder-width, hips and back into the pad, core braced.

Movement: Lower under control to a comfortable depth, then press up through the heels, keeping the knees soft at the top. The high foot position shifts more work to the hips and hamstrings.

Focus: Keep the knees tracking over the toes and the hips flat on the pad. Press through the heels, control the depth, and avoid rounding the lower back at the bottom.

Anterior tibialis – isometric hold
3 x 20 sec eachBodyweightBodyweight
Logged weight (if necessary)✓

Start: Sit on a chair with one leg out in front of you and the heel resting on the floor, knee softly bent. Everything above the ankle stays relaxed.

Movement: Pull the toes and the front of the foot up towards your shin and hold. Start with five seconds at about half effort and rest the same again. Six to eight holds is plenty.

Focus: This is the way back in for an ankle that has been out of action, where loaded raises are still too much. The first hint of a cramp is your ceiling, so work just underneath it and end the set there rather than pushing through. Half effort that never cramps beats full effort that does, and the ceiling moves within a fortnight.

Isometric shoulder press

Logged weight (if necessary)✓

Start: Stand or sit tall, arms bent with hands at shoulder height as if holding a press, ribs down, core braced.

Focus: Keep the ribs down and the lower back neutral, not arching to help. Keep the shoulders down away from the ears, and breathe steadily rather than holding your breath.

3 x 10BodyweightBodyweight
Logged weight (if necessary)✓

Dorsiflexion – banded

Start: Sit on the floor with the working leg straight out in front. Loop a resistance band around the ball of the foot and anchor the other end on something solid ahead of you, or hold it with both hands, so the band pulls the foot away from you.

Movement: Pull the toes and the front of the foot back towards the shin against the band, then let the band draw the foot slowly back out under control.

Focus: The front of the shin should be doing all the work, so keep the knee still and move only at the ankle. The slow return matters as much as the pull. Do each foot on its own so a weaker side cannot hide, and start with a light band, since this muscle tires fast. Loaded progression from anterior tibialis toe raises: once bodyweight raises are easy, this is where the strength comes from.

Romanian deadlift – BB

Start: Stand tall with a barbell held at the front of your thighs, hands just outside your legs, feet hip-width. Set the shoulders back, brace the core hard, and keep a soft bend in the knees.

Movement: Push the hips back and slide the bar down the front of your legs, keeping it in contact, until you feel a strong stretch in the hamstrings. Keep the knees still and the shins vertical. Drive the hips forward to stand tall and finish with the glutes.

Focus: The back stays flat from start to finish, never rounded. This is a hip hinge, not a squat, so the movement happens at the hips while the bar tracks close to the legs. Only lower as far as you can hold a flat back, and don't lean back or over-arch at the top.
