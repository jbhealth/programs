Cylinder squat – ViPR
3 sets8-10 repsViPRModerate
Did something different? Change Sets, Reps or Weight.

Start: Stand with feet hip to shoulder-width, holding the ViPR vertically on one end with both hands, arms relaxed, core braced.

Movement: Squat to a comfortable depth while keeping the ViPR balanced vertical, then drive up through the heels. Keep it steady throughout.

Focus: Balance the tool upright as you squat so the challenge is control, not load. Keep the chest tall, keep the knees in line with the middle toes, and move slowly so the balance demand stays high.

Romanian deadlift – trap bar
3 sets6-8 repsTrap barModerate
Did something different? Change Sets, Reps or Weight.

Start: Stand tall in the centre of the trap bar holding the handles at your sides, knees soft, chest up, shoulders back, core braced.

Movement: Push the hips back and let the bar travel down in front of the shins until you feel a stretch in the hamstrings, then drive the hips forward to stand tall. The neutral grip is kinder on the shoulders than a barbell.

Focus: Keep the back flat and the knees softly bent, never rounding to reach lower. Go only as low as the back stays flat, and feel it in the hamstrings, not the lower back.

Push press – DB
3 sets6-8 repsDBModerate
Did something different? Change Sets, Reps or Weight.

Start: Stand tall with a dumbbell in each hand at the shoulders, palms facing in, feet hip-width, ribs down, core and glutes braced.

Movement: Dip slightly at the knees, then drive up through the legs to help press the dumbbells overhead, then lower under control.

Focus: Use a short controlled leg dip and drive, keeping the ribs down and not arching the back on the press. Keep the knees in line with the middle toes, press both dumbbells evenly, and control the lower to the shoulders.

Hip adduction – cable
3 sets8-10 repsCable machineLight
Did something different? Change Sets, Reps or Weight.

Start: Stand side-on to a low cable with the ankle strap on the inside leg, holding the machine for support.

Movement: Sweep the working leg in across the front of the standing leg, then return slowly to the start. Finish the set, then change sides.

Focus: Stand tall on the outside leg and keep the hips level. Move the working leg slowly in both directions, without swinging.

Anterior tibialis – toe raises off a step
3 sets8-10 repsStep/BoxBodyweight
Did something different? Change Sets, Reps or Weight.

Start: Stand on a step or box with the heels on the surface and the toes hanging out over the edge. Hold a rail or frame for balance.

Movement: Let the toes lower below the level of the step under control, then pull them up towards the shins as high as they will go.

Focus: This is about range, not load. The edge lets the foot drop below level, which is where the shin has to work hardest and where most people have never trained. Go as low as the ankle allows before pulling back up, and keep the knees straight throughout.

Movement: Press through the balls of the feet and lift the heels, then lower slowly over three seconds. Progress to the edge of a step so the heels can drop below the toes.

Focus: Hold the knee bend the whole way. If the legs straighten as you rise, the work moves up into the bulky calf and away from the deep muscle you are after.

Squat – landmine
How to
3 x 6-8Moderate

Start: Hold the end of a landmine bar at your chest with both hands, feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out, core braced.

Movement: Sit the hips back and down into a squat, letting the bar's arc keep you upright, then drive up through the heels to stand tall.

Focus: Keep the knees in line with the middle toes and the heels down. The bar helps you stay upright, so keep the chest tall, and squat only as deep as the knees stay comfortable.

Bench press – BB
How to
3 x 6-8Moderate

Start: Lie on the bench, eyes under the bar, feet planted, shoulder blades pinched back and down, a slight natural arch. Grip a little wider than shoulders and unrack over the chest.

Movement: Lower the bar under control to the mid-chest, elbows tucked to about 45 degrees, then press it back up over the shoulders to straight arms.

Focus: Keep the shoulder blades pinched back and down throughout, which protects the shoulders, and the elbows tucked rather than flared wide. Keep the feet planted and the wrists stacked, lower under control to the chest, and don't bounce the bar.

Soleus – hold
How to
3 x 30sBodyweight

Start: Sit on a chair, feet flat, knees bent to about a right angle. Everything above the ankles relaxed.

Movement: Press the balls of the feet down, lift the heels and hold at about half effort. Build into the hold, and lower under control.

Focus: The gentlest way to load the deep calf, and it suits an ankle that is still being careful. No impact, so it can be done often. Little and often rather than hard.

Lateral walks – band, diagonal
How to
3 x 8-10Light

Start: Stand with a band around the legs, feet hip-width, knees softly bent, slight squat, torso tall.

Movement: Step diagonally forward and outward alternately, keeping tension on the band, for the set steps.

Focus: Keep tension on the band and the knees driving out, not caving. Stay low and controlled, and feel the outer hips working each step.

Dorsiflexion – band, slow lowering
How to
3 x 8-10Light

Start: Sit with the leg out in front and a band around the forefoot, anchored ahead of you. Use a heavier band than usual.

Movement: Pull the foot up towards the shin, using the other hand or foot to help if needed, then lower slowly against the band.

Focus: The lowering is the work. Take three or four seconds and resist the whole way. Because you help the foot up, the band can be heavier than the shin could lift alone.

Hamstring curl – Swiss ball, bilateral
How to
3 x 8-10Bodyweight

Start: Lie on your back, heels on a Swiss ball, legs straight, hips lifted so the body is in a straight line, arms flat.

Movement: Curl the ball towards the buttocks by bending the knees, keeping the hips lifted, then roll back out under control.

Focus: Keep the hips lifted and level, not dropping, and the ball steady. Keep the core braced, and control the roll back out.

Single leg deadlift – supported, hold
How to
3 x 30s each sideModerate

Start: Stand on one leg, soft knee, weight held in front of the thigh, fingertips on a chair back or worktop in front of you.

Movement: Hinge at the hip and lower the weight down the standing leg while the free leg lifts behind, reach a flat-backed stretch, then hold still.

Focus: Back flat, hips level, don't let the raised hip open out. Light fingertip support only, enough to steady you, and take the hand away when you feel steady.

Elbow plank – leg lift
How to
3 x 30sBodyweight

Start: Set up in a forearm plank, straight line from head to heels, core braced.

Movement: Keeping the hips level, lift one foot a few centimetres off the floor, hold briefly, lower, then the other.

Focus: Keep the hips level and square as the leg lifts, no rotating or sagging. Keep the ribs down and the core braced, and keep the lift small so the back stays neutral.

Golf ball release – foot
How to
1-2 min each footBodyweight

Start: Sit tall in a chair, or stand beside something solid to hold. Put a golf ball on the floor under the arch of one foot and ease your weight onto it gradually.

Movement: Press into a tender spot and hold until you feel it release, usually thirty to sixty seconds. When it lets go, find another. Two or three spots per foot is plenty.

Focus: Pressure, not pain. Settle onto the spot and stay still, because the tissue softens under a steady load rather than a rolling one. Breathe out slowly and let it go. Work the arch and the front of the heel, and ease off if the foot is sharply sore.

Hockey ball release – glute
How to
1-2 min each sideBodyweight

Start: Sit on the floor with a hockey ball under one buttock, hands behind you for support. Cross that ankle over the opposite knee to open the hip, and lower your weight on gradually.

Movement: Lean into a tender spot and hold until you feel it release, usually thirty to sixty seconds. When it lets go, shift and find another. Two or three spots per side.

Focus: Pressure you can breathe through, not pain. Hold still rather than rolling about, and take weight back through your hands if it is too much. If you get anything shooting, electric or numb down the leg you are on the nerve, so move off that spot straight away.

Sciatic nerve glide – seated
How to
10 slow each sideBodyweight

Start: Sit tall on a chair with both feet on the floor and your hands resting on your thighs.

Movement: Straighten one knee and pull the toes up towards you while you look up and lift the chin. Then bend the knee, point the toes and drop the chin towards your chest. Move slowly and smoothly between the two, one side at a time.

Focus: This slides the nerve rather than stretching it, so it should never reproduce the shooting pain down the leg. A mild pull behind the thigh is fine. Anything sharp, electric or numb means you have gone too far, so shorten the range until it is comfortable. Little and often beats hard and rarely, and if symptoms are worse afterwards rather than easier, leave it for the day.
