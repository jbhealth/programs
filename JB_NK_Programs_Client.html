How to run it
Straight sets: do all the sets of each exercise, rest, then move to the next.
Main Circuit
Leg press – bilateral
3 sets10-12 repsLeg press machine~70kg
Did something different? Change Sets, Reps or Weight.

Start: Sit in the leg press, feet flat and hip to shoulder-width on the platform, hips and back settled into the pad, core braced.

Movement: Lower the platform under control until the knees reach a comfortable bend, then press back up through the whole foot without locking out hard.

Focus: Keep the knees tracking in line with the toes, never caving in, and the hips flat against the pad. Control the depth to what the knees allow, and don't slam the knees straight at the top.

Lat pulldown
3 sets10-12 repsLat pulldown machine~32.5kg
Did something different? Change Sets, Reps or Weight.

Start: Sit at the machine, thighs under the pads, hands on the bar wider than shoulders, arms extended up, chest up.

Movement: Pull the bar down towards the upper chest by driving the elbows down, then return it up slowly under control.

Focus: Lead with the elbows and squeeze the shoulder blades down, not shrugging up. Keep the chest up and avoid leaning back to heave the bar, and control the return.

Chest press
3 sets10-12 repsAny kit/BenchModerate
Did something different? Change Sets, Reps or Weight.

Start: Lie on a bench, a weight in each hand at chest height, elbows a little below shoulder line, shoulder blades set back, feet planted.

Movement: Press the the weights up until the arms are nearly straight, then lower under control to a comfortable stretch.

Focus: Keep the shoulder blades set back on the bench and the elbows tucked to about 45 degrees, not flared wide, to protect the shoulders. Control the lower rather than dropping.

Glute kickback – cable
3 sets10 each sideCable machineLight
Did something different? Change Sets, Reps or Weight.

Start: Attach a cuff to your ankle at a low cable. Stand facing the machine, hold the frame for balance, slight bend in the standing knee.

Movement: Keeping the leg fairly straight, push it back behind you using the glute, then return with control. No swinging.

Focus: Move only at the hip and squeeze the glute at the back, don't arch the lower back to gain range. Keep the torso still and tall.

Balance board
3 sets30s each sideBalance boardBodyweight
Did something different? Change Sets, Reps or Weight.

Start: Stand on a balance board on one foot, knee soft, torso tall, near a support.

Movement: Keep the board level and steady on one leg, making constant small adjustments, for the set time.

Focus: Keep the standing knee soft and the hips level, adjusting from the ankle. Fix the eyes on a point, keep the core engaged, and keep support within reach.

Hamstring bridge – feet away
3 sets10 repsMatBodyweight
Did something different? Change Sets, Reps or Weight.

Start: Lie on your back and walk the feet further away than for a normal bridge, legs nearly straight. Heels down, toes lifted.

Movement: Drive through the heels and lift the hips into a line from shoulder to knee. Hold a moment, then lower slowly.

Focus: Moving the feet away shifts the work off the glutes and onto the hamstrings. If it cramps, walk the feet back in and build the distance out over a few weeks.

Goblet squat and reach

Start: Hold a weight at the chest, feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out.

Movement: Sit down into a squat and, at the bottom, reach the weight forward to help you sit upright, then draw it back to the chest and stand tall.

Focus: Keep the knees in line with the middle toes and the heels down. Use the reach to stay upright rather than tipping forward, and squat only as deep as the knees allow.

Good mornings – hugged

Start: Stand tall, feet hip-width, knees soft, hugging a weight to your chest with both arms, core braced.

Movement: Push the hips back and hinge forward with a flat back until the hamstrings load, then drive the hips forward to stand tall.

Focus: The weight sits high on the chest, so the back works hard to stay flat. Hinge at the hips, not the waist, and only go as far as that flat back allows.

3 x 10-12 · Moderate
Overhead press – seated

Start: Sit tall on a bench with back support, a weight in each hand at shoulder height, elbows under the wrists, ribs down.

Movement: Press the the weights overhead until the arms are straight, then lower them back to shoulder height under control.

Focus: Keep the ribs down and the back against the support, not arching to press. Keep the shoulders down away from the ears, and lower under control to a comfortable shoulder range.

3 x 10-12 · Moderate
Calf raises – eccentric heel drop

Start: Balls of both feet on the floor or the edge of a step, holding a support.

Movement: Rise onto the toes on both feet, lift one off, then lower the other heel slowly over three to four seconds. Foot down, rise on both again. One side at a time.

Focus: The slow lowering does the work, so give it the full count and do not let the heel drop away. Keep the ankle tracking straight, not rolling out.

3 x 10-12 · Bodyweight
Bicep curls

Start: Stand tall with a weight in each hand at the sides, or standing on a band holding the handles, elbows pinned to the ribs, ribs down, core braced.

Movement: Curl the weights up towards the shoulders, keeping the elbows still, then lower under control.

Focus: Keep the elbows pinned to the sides and don't swing the body to lift. Keep the ribs down, and control the lower rather than dropping.

Single leg deadlift – bodyweight

Start: Stand on one leg, torso tall, the other leg ready to travel back, standing knee soft, arms ready.

Movement: Hinge forward from the hip, letting the free leg travel back and the arms lower, then return to standing.

Focus: Hinge from the hip with a flat back, keeping the hips level and not opening out. Keep the standing knee soft, move slowly, and control the return.

3 x 10-12 each side · Bodyweight
Hip abduction machine

Start: Sit at the machine, the outer thighs against the pads, back supported, feet on the rests.

Movement: Push the thighs apart against the pads, then return slowly under control.

Focus: Push apart smoothly and control the return, not letting the pads snap the legs together. Keep the back settled and the movement steady, feeling the outer hips.

Elbow plank – leg lift

Start: Set up in a forearm plank, straight line from head to heels, core braced.

Movement: Keeping the hips level, lift one foot a few centimetres off the floor, hold briefly, lower, then the other.

Focus: Keep the hips level and square as the leg lifts, no rotating or sagging. Keep the ribs down and the core braced, and keep the lift small so the back stays neutral.

Goblet squat

Start: Hold a dumbbell or kettlebell close to your chest with both hands, elbows tucked down, feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out.

Movement: Sit the hips back and down into a squat, keeping the weight at the chest and the torso upright, then drive up through the heels to stand tall.

Focus: Keep the knees tracking over the toes, never caving in, and the heels flat. Keep the chest up and the weight close, and squat only as deep as you can control comfortably.

Deadlift – trap bar

Start: Stand in the centre of the trap bar, feet hip-width, the handles in line with the middle of your feet. Hinge down and grip the handles in the middle, chest up, back flat, core braced.

Movement: Push the floor away and stand tall, hips and shoulders rising together. Finish standing tall with the glutes, then sit the hips back and lower the bar under control to the floor.

Focus: The back stays flat from floor to lockout, never rounded. Push through the whole foot with the heels down, and stand tall at the top without leaning back.

Bench press – narrow grip

Start: Lie on the bench, feet planted, shoulder blades pinched back and down, hands about shoulder-width, unrack over the chest.

Movement: Lower the bar under control to the lower chest, elbows tucked close to the body, then press back up to straight arms.

Focus: Keep the elbows tucked close, which brings in the triceps and eases the shoulders, and the shoulder blades set. Keep the wrists stacked, lower under control, and don't let the elbows flare.

Focus: Lead with the elbows and draw the shoulder blades down and back, not shrugging. Keep the chest up and the torso still, and control the return.

ISO single leg deadlift – supported

Start: Stand on one leg, soft knee, weight held in front of the thigh, fingertips on a chair back or worktop in front of you.

Movement: Hinge at the hip and lower the weight down the standing leg while the free leg lifts behind, reach a flat-backed stretch, then hold still.

Focus: Back flat, hips level, don't let the raised hip open out. Light fingertip support only, enough to steady you, and take the hand away when you feel steady.

Dorsiflexion – banded eccentric

Start: Sit with the leg out in front and a band around the forefoot, anchored ahead of you. Use a heavier band than usual.

Movement: Pull the foot up towards the shin, using the other hand or foot to help if needed, then lower slowly against the band.

Focus: The lowering is the work. Take three or four seconds and resist the whole way. Because you help the foot up, the band can be heavier than the shin could lift alone.

Movement: Lift the hips so the body forms a straight line from head to heels and hold for the set time.

Focus: Keep the hips high and the body in one straight line, resisting the sag towards the floor. Keep the shoulder stacked over the elbow and the neck long, and stop when the line drops.

Movement: Sit the hips back and down to lightly touch the box, then drive up through the heels to stand tall, keeping the weight at the chest.

Focus: Keep the knees tracking over the toes and the heels down. Touch the box under control rather than dropping, and keep the chest up and the weight close throughout.

Romanian deadlift – trap bar

Start: Stand tall in the centre of the trap bar holding the handles at your sides, knees soft, chest up, shoulders back, core braced.

Movement: Push the hips back and let the bar travel down in front of the shins until you feel a stretch in the hamstrings, then drive the hips forward to stand tall. The neutral grip is kinder on the shoulders than a barbell.

Focus: Keep the back flat and the knees softly bent, never rounding to reach lower. Go only as low as the back stays flat, and feel it in the hamstrings, not the lower back.

Focus: Keep the back flat and the hips still, leading with the elbow and squeezing the shoulder blade. Don't round or twist the torso to lift, and control the lower.

Suitcase carry

Movement: Walk forward with controlled steps for the set distance, keeping the torso upright and level.

Focus: Resist the pull towards the weighted side, keeping the shoulders and hips level and the ribs down. Take steady steps, brace hard, and don't let the weight swing.

Anterior tibialis – toe raises off a step

Start: Stand on a step or box with the heels on the surface and the toes hanging out over the edge. Hold a rail or frame for balance.

Movement: Let the toes lower below the level of the step under control, then pull them up towards the shins as high as they will go.

Focus: This is about range, not load. The edge lets the foot drop below level, which is where the shin has to work hardest and where most people have never trained. Go as low as the ankle allows before pulling back up, and keep the knees straight throughout.

Rowing machine

Start: Sit with the feet strapped, knees bent, holding the handle, back tall, arms straight, leaning slightly forward.

Movement: Drive through the legs first, then lean back and pull the handle to the ribs, then reverse the sequence to return.

Focus: Drive with the legs first and keep the back tall, not rounding or yanking with the arms early. Keep the sequence smooth (legs, back, arms) and controlled, and build the pace gradually.

Golf ball release – foot

Start: Sit tall, or stand beside something solid to hold. Put a golf ball under the arch of one foot and ease your weight onto it.

Movement: Press into a tender spot and hold until it releases, usually thirty to sixty seconds. Then find another. Two or three spots per foot.

Focus: Pressure, not pain. Stay still, because the tissue softens under a steady load rather than a rolling one. Breathe out slowly. Ease off if the foot is sharply sore.

Hockey ball release – glute

Start: Sit on the floor with a hockey ball under one buttock, hands behind you. Cross that ankle over the opposite knee and lower your weight on gradually.

Movement: Lean into a tender spot and hold until it releases, thirty to sixty seconds. Two or three spots per side.

Focus: Pressure you can breathe through, not pain. Hold still rather than rolling about. Anything shooting, electric or numb down the leg is the nerve, so move off that spot straight away.

Sciatic nerve glide – seated

Start: Sit tall on a chair, both feet on the floor, hands on your thighs.

Movement: Straighten one knee and pull the toes up as you look up. Then bend the knee, point the toes and drop the chin to your chest. Slowly, one side at a time.

Focus: Slides the nerve rather than stretching it, so it should never reproduce the shooting pain down the leg. Anything sharp, electric or numb means too far, so shorten the range. Little and often.

Golf ball release – foot

Start: Sit tall in a chair, or stand beside something solid to hold. Put a golf ball on the floor under the arch of one foot and ease your weight onto it gradually.

Movement: Press into a tender spot and hold until you feel it release, usually thirty to sixty seconds. When it lets go, find another. Two or three spots per foot is plenty.

Focus: Pressure, not pain. Settle onto the spot and stay still, because the tissue softens under a steady load rather than a rolling one. Breathe out slowly and let it go. Work the arch and the front of the heel, and ease off if the foot is sharply sore.

1-2 min each foot · Bodyweight
Hockey ball release – glute

Start: Sit on the floor with a hockey ball under one buttock, hands behind you for support. Cross that ankle over the opposite knee to open the hip, and lower your weight on gradually.

Movement: Lean into a tender spot and hold until you feel it release, usually thirty to sixty seconds. When it lets go, shift and find another. Two or three spots per side.

Focus: Pressure you can breathe through, not pain. Hold still rather than rolling about, and take weight back through your hands if it is too much. If you get anything shooting, electric or numb down the leg you are on the nerve, so move off that spot straight away.

Sciatic nerve glide – seated

Start: Sit tall on a chair with both feet on the floor and your hands resting on your thighs.

Movement: Straighten one knee and pull the toes up towards you while you look up and lift the chin. Then bend the knee, point the toes and drop the chin towards your chest. Move slowly and smoothly between the two, one side at a time.

Focus: This slides the nerve rather than stretching it, so it should never reproduce the shooting pain down the leg. A mild pull behind the thigh is fine. Anything sharp, electric or numb means you have gone too far, so shorten the range until it is comfortable. Little and often beats hard and rarely, and if symptoms are worse afterwards rather than easier, leave it for the day.

Golf ball release – foot

Start: Sit tall in a chair, or stand beside something solid to hold. Put a golf ball on the floor under the arch of one foot and ease your weight onto it gradually.

Movement: Press into a tender spot and hold until you feel it release, usually thirty to sixty seconds. When it lets go, find another. Two or three spots per foot is plenty.

Focus: Pressure, not pain. Settle onto the spot and stay still, because the tissue softens under a steady load rather than a rolling one. Breathe out slowly and let it go. Work the arch and the front of the heel, and ease off if the foot is sharply sore.
