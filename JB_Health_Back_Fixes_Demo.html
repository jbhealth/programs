3 back fixes for the ache you train around

A free taster of how I coach

If your lower back nags by the end of the day, or grumbles when you bend and lift, the problem often isn't the back itself: it's what the back is being forced to cover for. Years of sitting leave the glutes quiet, the deep core switched off and the mid-back stiff. The lower back quietly ends up doing jobs that were never its to do, and that constant low-level overwork is what aches.

Here are three simple moves to start putting that right. No kit, nothing fancy, nothing you can't do on a bit of floor at home. Do them a few times a week and give them a fortnight before you judge them. This is about changing how the body moves, and that takes a little repetition. One thing first: if your back pain is sharp, spreading down a leg, or brand new, get it looked at properly before anything else. This is for the everyday niggle, not a red flag.

1
Wake the glutes up
Glute bridge

When your lower back nags, it often isn't weak, it's overworked. It has quietly taken on the job of your glutes, the big muscles meant to power your hips and protect your spine. Sit on them for years and they go quiet, and the back ends up doing their lifting all day long. No wonder it grumbles.

Start

Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet flat on the floor, roughly hip-width apart, heels a comfortable distance from your backside. Arms resting by your sides.

Movement

Drive through your heels and push your hips up until your body makes a straight line from shoulders to knees. Squeeze your glutes hard at the top and hold for two seconds, then lower slowly and with control. That slow lower is half the exercise.

Focus

Before you move, squeeze your backside and gently brace your core, as if bracing for a light prod to the stomach. The work should be felt in the glutes, not in your lower back or the backs of your thighs. If your back starts to grip, you've gone too high.

Do10–12 reps, 2–3 rounds. Quality beats height every time.

The point: This hands the work back to the muscle built for it. A glute that fires again is one that takes the daily strain off your lower back and puts it where it belongs.

2
Switch the deep core on
Dead bug

Your deep core is the natural corset around your middle. Its job is to keep your spine steady while your arms and legs move. When it isn't doing that job, every reach, lift and twist asks your lower back to hold things together instead, and that constant bracing is a classic driver of the nagging ache.

Start

Lie on your back with your knees bent up over your hips and your shins level with the floor, so the knees are stacked above the hips. Reach both arms straight up toward the ceiling.

Movement

Slowly lower your right arm back overhead and straighten your left leg toward the floor at the same time, going only as far as you can while keeping your back flat. Bring them back and swap sides. Slow and controlled, breathing out as you extend.

Focus

Press your lower back gently down into the floor so there's no gap underneath it, and keep it there. Brace your core lightly. That flat-back contact is the whole exercise. The moment your back arches up off the floor you've lost it, so make the movement smaller.

Do5–8 each side, 2–3 rounds. Stop short of where your back lifts.

The point: This teaches the deep core to do its real job, holding your spine steady so your back isn't bracing every time you move. That's stability your back can finally rely on.

3
Free up the stiff mid-back
Open book

Your lower back isn't built to twist much. Your mid-back is. But years of sitting stiffen the mid-back up, and when you turn to reach or look behind you, the lower back is forced to do the rotating it was never meant to. Give the mid-back its movement back and you take that demand off the spine below.

Start

Lie on your side with your knees bent up toward you, hips and knees stacked, and both arms stretched out in front of you at shoulder height, palms together.

Movement

Keeping your knees down, slowly sweep your top arm up and over like opening a book, following your hand with your eyes, until it reaches toward the floor on the other side. Breathe out as you open, feel the gentle turn through the mid-back, pause, then close slowly. Repeat, then swap sides.

Focus

Keep your knees down and together throughout, a cushion between them helps. The movement comes from your upper back opening, not from your hips rolling. Keeping the knees pinned is what sends the turn to the right place.

Do6–8 each side, slow and easy. Never force the turn.

The point: A mid-back that rotates freely means your lower back stops being dragged into jobs it can't do safely. Less twisting strain on the spine, and easy movement where it actually belongs.

A word from me

These three won't fix everything, but they'll show you something worth knowing: most aches in a well-used body aren't actually where the pain is. They're upstream, in how the body moves and which muscles have gone quiet. Fix the cause and the symptom very often quietens down on its own.
