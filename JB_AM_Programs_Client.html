Program 1 — Knee-Dominant Strength & Stability
Knee-dominant strength · Single-leg stability · Anti-rotation core
Main Circuit
Single Leg RDL (Knee-Dominant)
3 x 8 each · Light-ModerateDumbbell

Start: Stand on one leg with a soft knee. Hold a dumbbell in the opposite hand (or both hands). Your standing leg should feel engaged but not locked.

Focus: Keep your hips square. The movement comes from a hip hinge—push your hips back while maintaining a slight knee bend on your standing leg. Feel your hamstring and glute working.

Movement: Hinge at the hip, extending the non-standing leg behind you for counterbalance. Lower the dumbbell toward the ground on a diagonal in front of you. Drive through the heel and glute to return to standing. Maintain a neutral spine throughout.

Bulgarian Split Squat (Rear Foot Elevated)
3 x 8 each · Light-ModerateDumbbell

Start: Face away from a bench or step. Place the top of one foot on the bench behind you. Your front foot should be far enough forward that when you squat, your front knee stays over your ankle. Hold dumbbells at your sides or in a rack position.

Focus: Keep your torso upright. The working leg is the front leg—ensure your front shin stays relatively vertical. Drive through the front heel.

Movement: Squat down, bending the front knee until the rear knee nearly touches the ground. The front knee should track over your toes. Drive back up through the front heel. Complete all reps on one side, then switch.

Suitcase Carry
3 x 40m each side · ModerateKettlebell or Dumbbell

Start: Stand tall holding a dumbbell or kettlebell in one hand at your side. Engage your core. Your shoulders should stay level.

Focus: Walk with posture—no leaning. Feel your core bracing against the one-sided load. Keep your shoulders packed (not shrugging).

Movement: Walk for the prescribed distance (or time) carrying the weight. Resist the urge to tilt toward the weight. Switch hands and repeat.

Single-Leg Balance Reach (Multi-Directional)
3 x 6-8 each directionBodyweight

Start: Stand on one leg with a soft knee. Arms out to the sides for balance if needed.

Focus: Keep your standing leg stable. Move smoothly through all directions. The goal is dynamic balance, not static—keep moving, keep thinking.

Movement: Reach forward with one arm (or tap the ground in front), return to center. Reach to the side, return. Reach behind, return. Move through these directions in a controlled, flowing manner. Switch legs and repeat.

Pallof Press (Half-Kneeling)
3 x 8 each side · Light-ModerateCable or Band

Start: Half-kneel (one knee down, one foot forward) perpendicular to a cable machine set at chest height. Hold the cable handle at chest with both hands, cable coming from the side. Brace your core.

Focus: Resist rotation. Your torso should not twist. The press is a test of stability—the weight isn't the point, controlling the rotation is.

Movement: Press the handle straight out in front of you (or slightly up). Feel your core resist the rotational pull from the cable. Return the handle to chest. Complete all reps, then switch sides.

Calf Raise (Single Leg)
3 x 10 each · Bodyweight or LightBodyweight or Light Dumbbell

Start: Stand on one leg. You can hold a wall or rail for light balance support if needed. Your standing leg should be straight but not locked.

Focus: Move through the full range of motion—from a slight dorsiflexion (heel down) to full plantar flexion (up on the ball of your foot). Keep your body controlled.

Movement: Rise up onto the ball of your foot, lifting your heel as high as it will go. Pause briefly at the top. Lower back down with control. Complete all reps on one side, then switch.

Step-Down with Rotation
3 x 8 each side · BodyweightStep Box (8-12")

Start: Stand on a step box (8-12" high) with one foot near the edge and the other leg hanging off the side.

Focus: Control the lowering phase (eccentric). As the hanging leg descends, allow your torso to rotate gently toward that side. Keep your standing knee soft and aligned over your toes.

Movement: Step down with a gentle twist—as one leg lowers to tap the ground, your torso rotates in that direction. Return to standing by driving through the stepping leg. Complete all reps, then switch sides.

Glute Bridge (Single Leg)
3 x 10 each · Bodyweight or LightBodyweight or Light Dumbbell

Start: Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat on the floor. Your feet should be positioned roughly hip-width apart, directly under your knees.

Focus: Drive through the heel of your working leg. Feel your glute and hamstring (not your lower back) doing the work. Keep your hips level—don't let one side dip.

Movement: Push your hips toward the ceiling, lifting one foot slightly off the ground so all the load is on the other leg. Hold at the top briefly, squeezing your glute. Lower with control. Complete all reps on one side, then switch.

Reverse Lunge to Knee Raise
3 x 8 each · Light-ModerateDumbbell

Start: Stand tall with feet hip-width apart. Arms can be at your sides or crossed in front of your chest.

Focus: Step backward far enough that your front knee can bend to approximately 90 degrees. Keep your torso upright and core braced.

Movement: Step backward with one leg, lowering into a lunge until your rear knee nearly touches the ground. Drive through your front heel to stand back up. As you stand, raise the rear leg's knee up in front of you (hip flexion). Lower that leg and repeat, or step backward again with the other leg. This creates a dynamic stepping and balancing challenge.

Dead Bug with Knee Drive
3 x 8 each sideBodyweight

Start: Lie on your back with arms extended straight up toward the ceiling. Hips and knees bent at 90 degrees (knees directly over hips).

Focus: Keep your lower back flat on the floor throughout. Move slowly and deliberately. The challenge is maintaining stability as you extend limbs.

Movement: Extend one arm overhead while straightening the opposite leg, lowering both toward the floor without touching. Return to the starting position. Repeat on the other side (opposite arm, opposite leg). This alternating pattern challenges core stability and knee control.
